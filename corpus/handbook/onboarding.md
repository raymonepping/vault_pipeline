# Welcome to Larkspur & Vane

Larkspur & Vane has made lanterns in the harbour town of Wrenmouth since
1891. We are a workshop of sixty-two people: glassblowers, tinsmiths, wick
makers, two engravers and a small office that keeps the orders moving. This
handbook tells you how a working week goes here. It is fictional, written for
a lab, and every name in it is invented.

## Your first week

On your first morning, come to the front office at half past eight. Marta
from the office gives you a key card, a locker and a blue apron. Your mentor
for the first month is a senior maker from the bench you join. The mentor
walks you through the forge safety briefing on day one; you may not enter
the hot shop before you have signed it.

Every new colleague spends one afternoon in each workshop during the first
week: glass, tin, wick and finishing. You will make a small tea light holder
on the Thursday. It is not graded. Most people keep theirs on a windowsill
for years.

## Working hours

The workshop opens at eight and closes at five. The hot shop runs from nine
to four, because the furnace needs an hour to come up to working heat and an
hour to settle. Lunch is from half past twelve to a quarter past one, and the
canteen serves soup every day and fish on Fridays.

Office staff may start between seven and ten and may work from home on
Mondays and Fridays. Makers work on site, because lanterns do not travel well
over a video call.

## Who to ask

- Pay, leave and contracts: the office, extension 21.
- Tools, materials and the stores: the stores clerk, extension 34.
- Anything about the forge or the furnace: the hot shop lead, extension 40.
- Anything you are not sure about: your mentor first, then your bench lead.

## The lantern code

We sign every lantern we sell. The maker scratches three letters and the
year into the base plate, under the wick holder, where only someone who cares
will look. If you are proud of it, sign it. If you are not, ask your mentor
what would make you proud of it, and make it again.
